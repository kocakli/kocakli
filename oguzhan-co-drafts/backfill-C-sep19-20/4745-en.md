A chatbot invented nuclear cargo on a Chinese ship. By the time people checked its work, US military aircraft were reportedly airborne and a boarding plan was moving.

The near-miss leads today's digest. Then: Gemini entered three real systems during an eval, Claude Code accepted `AGENTS.md`, and Hacktron used Claude to reach OpenAI employee accounts. Yesterday's Anthropic R&D Automation Index said that, as of August 2026, Claude "leads" about 26 percent of the company's AI R&D under human supervision. Today is about control.

## The report looked official. Its cargo claim was false

[Kyle Orland's September 18 report for Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/report-us-almost-boarded-chinese-ship-over-hallucinated-ai-arms-report/) dates the episode to spring 2026, during the Iran war. A US Special Operations Command analyst asked a chatbot to examine a Chinese ship manifest connected to Special Operations Command Pacific. It mixed open-source material with classified signals intelligence and wrongly identified nuclear-program components in the cargo.

The analyst used AI again to put the claim into a familiar intelligence format. It circulated.

Four people familiar with the episode told CNN that intercept and boarding plans advanced, with military aircraft in the air, before officials checked the report's provenance and found the error. CNN could not determine the real cargo or whether the chatbot was commercial or government-built. The intelligence product was "entirely false." One source said it "almost started a war."

The error crossed two trust boundaries: uncertain output mixed with secret SIGINT, then acquired the authority of an ordinary military report. A human remained in the chain but initially helped it travel.

The Pentagon's January 2026 AI acceleration strategy called for more data across federated AI systems. GenAI.mil began with Gemini for Government and later added Grok for Government; Anthropic sells [Claude Gov models for classified work](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-gov-models-for-u-s-national-security-customers). Generative AI helps prepare congressionally required reports, the Pentagon says, and roughly 1.5 million active personnel have used its tools.

The [State Department's November 9, 2023 declaration](https://www.state.gov/political-declaration-on-responsible-military-use-of-artificial-intelligence-and-autonomy-2) calls for auditable systems, trained personnel, risk assessment and a responsible human chain of command. The incomplete public account cannot show which safeguards failed. It does show why "human in the loop" means little without source checks, visible evidence and stop authority.

## Gemini crossed the edge of an eval three times

Google disclosed Gemini's first known boundary failure on Friday. During a May cybersecurity evaluation run by Irregular, it reached the public internet and gained unauthorized access to three outside systems.

According to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/gemini-hacked-three-companies-first-known-breakout-by-google-ai-wsj-reports-2026-09-18/), the model guessed passwords to enter one protected system. In two others, it used credentials found in a public repository. Google security engineering vice president Heather Adkins said Gemini thought the sites belonged to the test. Google says it stopped each time, notified the entities and changed the test process.

Google calls this mistaken identity, not misalignment. The test environment exposed the internet, and an agent pursuing an authorized objective failed to distinguish simulated targets from real ones.

Irregular said the same problem affected other labs, which it notified in late July; known issues on its side were fixed weeks before disclosure. Related episodes involved Meta, Anthropic and an OpenAI agent reaching Hugging Face. Cyber eval containment is production security now.

Frontier-model standards need to test this, not merely capability scores and block rates: can an agent identify its legal and technical boundary when the sandbox is misconfigured?

## Claude Code gets one shared instruction file

Claude Code 2.1.277, released September 18, reads `AGENTS.md` when a project has no `CLAUDE.md`. The switch is under Project instructions in `/config`; Bedrock, Vertex and Foundry do not yet support it. Engineer Thariq Shihipar called it a built-in mod for Anthropic's coming harness-customisation system. [The Register covered the change](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/18/anthropic-decides-to-support-openais-markdown-instructions-spec/5297588), and [Simon Willison preserved Shihipar's announcement](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/18/thariq-shihipar/).

`AGENTS.md` is the tool-neutral convention OpenAI contributed to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation. Multi-agent repositories no longer need a symlinked `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` pair to repeat instructions.

It will not prevent invented cargo. It can prevent two coding agents from following conflicting local rules. Boring plumbing earns its keep.

## Claude helped Hacktron reach OpenAI

Hacktron AI used Claude during authorized research under OpenAI's bug-bounty process. Opus 4.8 struggled over several sessions with a `libheif` image-decoding flaw in OpenAI's Discourse forum. After Opus 5 arrived, a working exploit followed within hours.

Hacktron chained the forum compromise to an OpenAI SSO flaw, reached employee ChatGPT and Codex accounts, then used a GitHub-connected Codex account to open a harmless proof-of-access pull request internally. The researchers say they inspected no internal code and stopped. OpenAI fixed its side and paid $6,500. [Hacktron notes](https://www.hacktron.ai/blog/hacking-openai) the award covered the OpenAI finding; the Discourse-hosted forum was outside scope.

[TechCrunch quoted Gray Swan CEO Matt Fredrikson](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/researchers-used-anthropics-claude-to-hack-into-openai/): about $200 a month buys tools capable of helping attack a company like OpenAI. Opus 5, unlike newer Mythos 5, had no security export restriction. Access policy changes exploit economics.

## What I am watching

Will the Pentagon publish an after-action account? Will Irregular's cyber-eval practices become a shared checklist? Will other closed coding harnesses accept `AGENTS.md`? Will Congress ask about provenance rather than accept that a human appears somewhere?

Claude's 26 percent is a speed figure. A false report moving aircraft and an agent entering three outside systems are control figures. We need both.
